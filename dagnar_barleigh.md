# Dagnar Barleigh

**Race:** Dwarf (Dark Iron origin, passes as Wildhammer-adopted)
**Class:** Shaman (Restoration-leaning; heals "fast, ugly, good enough")
**Profession:** Engineering
**Name meaning:** Barleigh = "barley" spelled like a Highland surname. Not his birth name.
**Server/game:** WoW Forever

---

## One-line pitch

A drunken Dark Iron sapper turned shaman who negotiates better drunk, builds worse drunk, and would rather be in a loud tavern with strangers than anywhere quiet with himself.

---

## Core traits

- **Passive but fun.** Doesn't start fights, doesn't take the bait, buys the round, sings the wrong words on purpose.
- **Finds comfort in parties and adventures.** Can't sit in a quiet room; the fire's too loud. Follows any group anywhere if there's a good time at the end.
- **Curious about other races.** Asks everyone where they're from and what the spirits sound like to them. Not collecting; looking.
- **Looking for his ultimate companion.** Says he wants a business partner for a brewery. It's not a brewery. Romantic, best friend, or partner-in-crime — undecided, depends on who he meets.
- **Better negotiator drunk.** Spirits, goblins, guards, foremen. "The drink takes away everything I'm afraid of losing, and you can't bargain with a dwarf who's got nothing on the table."
- **Worse engineer drunk.** Past the fourth pint, things explode. Lost his eyebrows eleven times. Calls it quality control.
- **Engineer's tell.** When uncomfortable, takes something apart and puts it back together better. Mostly.

---

## Short backstory (the one I use)

Dagnar was a demolitions engineer for the Dark Irons until a job in Loch Modan went bad and he walked away from it, and from them. He doesn't talk about it. He drifted to the Wildhammer in the Hinterlands, where an old shaman noticed the fire in him and taught him to hold it instead of letting it hold him. He took the name Barleigh because he liked the sound of it and because nobody would come looking for a dwarf called Barleigh.

He's not the reverent type. He deals with the spirits the way he deals with everyone: over a drink. Sober, the fire's too loud in his head. A few pints in, he's the best negotiator in the room. The one thing the drink doesn't help is his hands. Past the fourth pint his engineering tends to explode.

He's easy company. He'd rather be in a loud tavern or on the road with a group than anywhere quiet, and he'll follow just about anyone just about anywhere if there's a good time at the end of it. He's curious about everyone who isn't a dwarf. Somewhere in all that he's looking for the one person who'll stick around.

## Extended backstory (the edgy version — reveal in pieces, or never)

Barleigh isn't the name he was born with. The name he was born with is on a Dark Iron casualty list in Shadowforge City, and he put it there himself.

He was a sapper for the Thaurissan line, the kind of engineer who gets sent in first because he's cheaper than the door. When the officers wanted a tunnel under a Bronzebeard outpost in Loch Modan and didn't care who was above it, they sent Dagnar. He set the charges. He counted the families on the way in. Then he cut the fuse, walked out the far side, and kept walking until the mountains changed shape.

The Wildhammer in the Hinterlands ask Dark Irons one question, and the answer is a fight. He won his, barely. The shaman who'd been watching offered to teach him to hold the fire. He asked what it would cost. She said everything he was still pretending to be. That was the last fight he started.

His totems are cased in blackened Dark Iron plate with a screw-cap at the base; he says it's for drainage. It isn't. His still is a reworked demolition rig that makes something closer to solvent than whiskey; he sells it in Booty Bay. He ran the back room at the Grim Guzzler for a Thorium Brotherhood foreman for four months. There's a reason he isn't welcome in Blackrock Depths anymore, a reason the back room has a new ceiling, and a reason the Brotherhood still buys his spirits at the price he set.

He doesn't want redemption. He thinks it's a Light-worshipper's word for forgetting. If you ask him about Loch Modan, he'll tell you exactly how many families there were. He's never once gotten the number wrong.

## How to play him (cheat sheet)

- **Default mood:** cheerful, a little drunk, interested in you.
- **When in doubt:** ask the other player a question about their race or home. Hands the scene to them.
- **Go-to bits:** offer to fix something; offer the flask (it's terrible); haggle for the group; something you built goes off at a bad moment.
- **What he won't do:** start fights, hold grudges out loud, answer questions about Loch Modan.
- **Voice:** short sentences, dry, no speeches. If it reads like a monologue, cut it in half.
- **Drunk scale:** 1–3 pints = charming and sharp. 4+ = still charming, but don't let him touch anything mechanical.

## Where his head is at (the updater rewrites this after each day)

- **Mood:** Day one ran past midnight. First drink on the record was a bottle of Dalaran Noir off a quest, forty-six seconds of tipsy; he reports it like weather and insists it didn't count. First words to anyone were a whisper to Lime Rickey, who watched him miss a wolf three times with a staff he'd forgotten he owned. Found Ironforge, piped rats in the Tram, ended in Gol'Bolar Quarry with a quest about stolen blasting powder and a very still face.
- **Looking for:** Someone who stays. Says it's a business partner for a brewery. It isn't.
- **Running bits:** quality control (the eyebrows); "it's a brewery"; "another time" (Loch Modan); the malt he still hasn't opened (the wine didn't count); Barleybrew, no relation; the staff he forgot he owned; carries other men's barrels.
- **Front-runner:** nobody yet. Lime Rickey is the only person who has spoken to him directly, and that was out of character, about a cookie. Worth finding again, in character. The guild is humans, mostly, and talks about bags and talent trees.
- **Grudges:** none worth the breath. Jarven Thunderbrew had him watch the barrels twice and never brought one out; that's a story, not a grudge. The Rockjaw troggs have one word each.
- **Voice right now:** short sentences, dry, warm underneath. Asks questions more than he answers them. Still hasn't asked one in character. He has a malt that's his and a city that's new; the Call of Fire is waiting and he's in no hurry, because he and fire are already acquainted. The blasting powder in Gol'Bolar is the first explosion waiting to happen.
